## 9:35–10:35 · The Secret Path

**9:35–9:40:** Explain that Chanie Wenjack was an Anishinaabe child who died in 1966 while trying to reach his family after leaving a residential school. The film is Gord Downie and Jeff Lemire's artistic retelling, not Chanie's own testimony. Say the story includes harm to children, separation and death. Students may pause, draw a detail, listen without speaking or use the quiet option.

**About 9:40–10:20:** Show only the preview-selected coherent opening chapters. Stop at the noted chapter boundary by about 10:20. The previous 46-minute suggestion cannot fit here with context, snack and a debrief. Record the point to resume another day; do not imply the class saw the complete story.

**10:20–10:35:** Snack and a calm response. Ask two or three of: What is one detail we should remember? How did image, colour, word or sound communicate meaning? What could our own artwork communicate? A student may point, sketch, dictate or remain quietly reflective. Do not ask for personal disclosure, reenactment, or a prescribed feeling. Distinguish the historical facts from the film's artistic choices.

## 10:50–11:50 · Orange Shirt Day art

Revisit the [Orange Shirt Society's account by Phyllis Webstad](https://orangeshirtday.org/about/phyllis-story/): at age six, the orange shirt from her grandmother was taken at residential school. This is **Phyllis's distinct story** and the origin of Orange Shirt Day; do not merge it with Chanie's story. The [NCTR history page](https://nctr.ca/education/residential-school-history/) gives wider system context. Guiding question: **What do we want people to understand and remember through our Orange Shirt Day artwork?**
