Pieces → wholes

Same amount. Different names. Work on your own or with a partner. You need a pencil; colouring or cutting is optional. No device or upload needed.

First notice: One whole divided into four equal parts is 4/4 = 1. All the whole strips below are the same size. Do not count empty spaces as part of your amount.

1. Build seven quarters

Shade 7 quarter-pieces across these strips. Outline each complete whole. Write both number names, then explain what stayed the same.

Another way to show the same amount: You can mark seven quarters on this number line. Each small interval is one quarter. Each space between whole numbers matches one whole strip.

7/4 = ______ whole(s) and ______ fourths = __________

2. Work backwards

Shade 2 whole strips and 1 third of another. Count the thirds altogether. Explain why the denominator is still 3.

2⅓ = ______ thirds = ______ / 3

3. Test your idea

On the back, draw equal strips to rename 11/4 and 8/4. Does every improper fraction need a fractional part in its other name? Prove your answer with the model.

Go further: Build 6/4. Explain why 1 2/4 and 1 1/2 name the same amount. Invent two improper fractions between 1 and 2.

You can explain by talking, pointing, drawing or writing. A finished response shows the same amount in a model and two number names. Keep it for discussion; no upload required.
