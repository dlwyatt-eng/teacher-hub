PHE health - Week 1

A way back into the game

Change a barrier while respecting a boundary.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional game: after a mistake, players must run a long loop. One player asks to walk a short return. The captain refuses. Someone starts pulling the player without asking. The goal is passing practice. Barrier: something blocking participation. Boundary: a limit to respect.

Look at the example

Example: A partner offers a hand. The learner says no. The partner accepts no and asks, "What would help?" Help needs permission.

1. Point to a rule or action blocking participation. What boundary matters?

2. Choose or design one change. Show how passing practice continues.

  • Short return route
  • Closer passing space
  • Another safe idea

3. What could the group ask or notice to check whether the change helped?

  • Could you rejoin?
  • Did you have choices?
  • Was the space safe?

Words you can use

We can change ___ because ___.

Please ask before ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

PHE health - Week 2

Pause, choose, get support

Make a flexible reset-and-help plan.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional case: a player misses two passes, grips the ball and says, "I cannot do this." They ask for a pause. A teammate wants to restart now. The teacher is busy.

Look at the example

Example: A drawing tears. The learner pauses, tries a quiet count, then asks an adult for help. A strategy can change; returning is not forced.

  1. Pause
  2. Choose
  3. Return or get help

1. Point to a clue in the case. What should the teammate respect?

2. Choose a first action and a strategy. Explain why they fit.

  • Pause in the agreed place
  • Use a quiet count
  • Look at one task cue
  • Ask a trusted adult

3. The strategy does not help and the teacher is busy. Show a backup.

  • Another available trusted adult
  • More space and adult support

Words you can use

The case says ___.

First ___. If needed, ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

PHE health - Week 3

Change one part of a plan

Fit a movement plan to the available time and space.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional case: the outdoor route is unavailable. A learner has ten minutes and a clear indoor space. Nothing needs buying. FITT: frequency = how often; intensity = how hard; time = how long; type = which activity.

Look at the example

Example: A five-minute walk becomes five minutes of seated reaching and tapping. Type changes; time stays. The learner can pause and adjust.

1. Choose an indoor activity. Why does it fit this case?

  • Short walk or wheel route
  • Seated reach and tap
  • Four-move rhythm pattern

2. Name one FITT change. Add a private check and a rest, stop or help option.

3. Now only five minutes remain. Revise the plan and explain your choice.

Words you can use

I changed ___ because ___.

If needed, the learner can ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

PHE health - Week 4

A claim needs evidence

Check a claim and protect private information.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional ad: NeonFocus Gear sells PowerBand. "Everyone feels calm and scores better after seven days! Proof: 200 likes and one anonymous review. Post your face photo and daily energy score." No study is named. Source capsule: BC Women's Hospital + Health Centre (PHSA), Evaluating Online Health Info, paraphrased: check author, qualifications, purpose, review date and research. Start with established health organizations, such as HealthLink BC. This guidance does not test PowerBand. Claim = what a message says is true. Evidence = checkable information supporting a claim.

Look at the example

Example: An ad calls a shoe "best for every sport." I can identify the seller. I still need checkable evidence for that claim.

1. Point to the seller, the claim and the offered proof. What is missing?

2. What does the real source help you check? What can it not prove?

3. Choose a safe next step. What stays private?

  • Pause
  • Ask a trusted adult
  • Check a reliable source

Words you can use

The claim is ___. We still need ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

PHE health - Week 5

Question the message; respect people

Challenge a body-rating message without judging bodies.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional post: a sportswear seller calls an edited picture "the only athletic look" and invites body ratings. The small print says the image was edited. A group chat repeats the invitation. No real photograph is needed.

Look at the example

Example: A shoe seller says one shoe suits every sport. I ask which sports were tested. Selling something is a reason to check, not proof either way.

1. Which facts can the post support? Which personal judgments can it not establish?

  • Clothing is for sale
  • The picture was edited
  • A person's health
  • A person's worth

2. Replace the rating invitation with two welcoming lines. Explain one word choice.

3. Targeting continues. Choose a boundary or help response and explain it.

  • Ask to stop body ratings
  • Leave the chat and seek help
  • Tell a trusted adult

Words you can use

Our message could say ___.

If this continues, ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

PHE health - Week 6

A help path with a backup

Protect first, get adult help and follow up.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional message: an unknown account offers a sports-team place. It asks for a face photo, home address and private health details: "Keep this secret. Meet alone." The teacher will confirm where school helpers can be found.

Look at the example

Different case: a practice area is unsafe and someone is hurt. Move away from danger and alert the supervising adult now. Do not investigate or diagnose.

  1. Protect now
  2. Get adult help
  3. Use a backup

1. For the message case, choose protection now. Explain one choice.

  • Do not reply or send details
  • Do not go to the meeting
  • Tell a trusted adult privately

2. Choose a helper and confirmed place. Say or show a short help sentence. Who is the backup?

  • Classroom teacher
  • Supervising adult
  • School office adult

3. What changes if there is immediate danger?

Words you can use

I need help with ___.

If ___ is unavailable, I can ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

Reusable PE - Partner cues

Ready, send, receive, reset

Make the next pass playable and communicate clearly.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Use a teacher-taught partner activity. Teacher supplies safe space and suitable equipment. Fresh problem: both partners signal ready, but the ball keeps going beyond the receiver. Point, speak or draw a plan before practising.

Look at the example

Example: a receiver is not ready. The sender waits for a ready signal, then makes a gentle roll. The receiver controls it. They pause before the next turn.

  1. Ready
  2. Send
  3. Receive
  4. Reset

1. For the fresh problem, choose one teacher-approved change. Why?

  • Move closer
  • Send more gently
  • Use a suitable larger/light ball
  • Another safe idea

2. Practise three exchanges through your agreed movement route. Name what you actually used.

  • Roll
  • Foot pass
  • Catch and send
  • Forearm pass

3. What changed? Show the agreed pause or help signal.

Words you can use

The next ball was ___ because ___.

Please pause. I need ___.

Pause, please.

Help, please.

Reusable PE - Captain cues

Choose one change, then replay

Use clear instructions and evidence to improve familiar practice.

Listen or read. Point, speak, draw or write. Fictional Gate Pass group: players crowd one gate and spend long periods waiting. They want safe passing turns and choices. Select only a change the teacher approves. Captains lead familiar practice while the teacher supervises.

Look at the example

Different example: two receivers chase one ball. The team agrees a receiving signal, replays, then checks whether space stayed clear. One observation does not settle every question.

  1. Notice
  2. Choose one change
  3. Replay
  4. Decide

1. Choose or design one change for the gate problem. Predict its effect.

  • Change gate spacing
  • Use smaller practice groups
  • Another safe idea

2. Give a short instruction for the changed game. Include a stop and return cue.

  • Our practice goal is...
  • Freeze means...
  • You can rejoin by...

3. After replay, choose a decision. Use one observation and player feedback.

  • Keep
  • Revise
  • Reject
  • Try again

Words you can use

We noticed ___. We changed ___.

We would ___ because ___.
