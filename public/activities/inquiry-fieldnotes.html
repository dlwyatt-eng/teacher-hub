My starting idea

A claim is an idea you can check. You do not need to be certain before you begin.

2A

Evidence from one place

Record title, author or organization, link or page, publication date (or not given) and date checked.

Use your own words or a short quotation in quotation marks. Name the people, place and time described.

2B

Evidence from the other

Record title, author or organization, link or page, publication date (or not given) and date checked.

Compare carefully: are the dates, groups or measures different?

A little help, not the answer

Explain the words

Claim: an idea that can be checked. Evidence: a detail that helps us judge an idea. Perspective: how someone's experience and position shape what they notice. Limit: what the evidence cannot tell us.

Show me a small example

Invented practice example, not research about a real country: A town report says a new bus route serves three schools. A student interview says that student's trip became shorter.

Too broad: “Everyone can get to school easily now.”

Better supported: “The new route may help some students. One student describes a shorter trip, but I need evidence about cost, accessibility and areas the route does not serve.”

Your inquiry needs real, traceable sources from both countries. A generated answer is not a source.

I would rather talk or draw

Fold a paper into four sections. Label them idea → evidence → missing → thinking now. Sketch the comparison and label source details. Or talk through the four sections with a partner who asks, “What makes you say that?” Keep a paper record or explain to your teacher. No recording is needed.

Go further

Find a second, independent source that might challenge your answer. Does it cover the same date, place and group? Explain why one source is more useful for your particular question. More sources are not automatically better evidence.

Ready to explain?

Finished product: a short comparison you can explain, with source notes and an honest limit. Keep this with your inquiry work. It is not an extra required SpacesEDU post.
