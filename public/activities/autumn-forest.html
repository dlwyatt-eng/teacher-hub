Make a forest with depth and mood

How can colour, size and overlap make a flat page feel like a place we could enter?

Start here

Look at the finished example in step 4. Which tree seems closest? Point to the size, colour or overlap that creates that effect. Decide what you want your forest to feel like.

Materials: white paper, oil pastels or pencil crayons, brown and dark paper scraps, scissors and glue. Pale paper is optional. Draw layers instead of cutting, use pre-cut strips, or ask for cutting support.

Your finished piece: a layered landscape and a short explanation of one choice that creates depth or mood. Your palette and arrangement can differ from the example.

1 · Background2 · Distant trees3 · Middle trees4 · Foreground

1 · Make the background

Build bands of colour across the sky. Blend gently where they meet.

Sketch a distant hill. If you want a path, make it narrow near the horizon and wider toward the bottom.

Think: the light horizon draws the eye. Would a different colour or contrast create the mood you want?

2 · Place distant trees

Draw small, pale trees with pastel, or cut thin shapes from light paper.

Leave space between some trees so the background still shows through.

Try: compare a small tree with a larger one. Which feels farther away? What else affects that impression?

3 · Add middle trees

Cut medium brown trunks. Separate narrow strips can become branches.

Move the pieces before gluing. Let some overlap distant trees without covering the whole sky.

Decide: where should the viewer look first? Try two arrangements and choose the one that supports your idea.

4 · Finish the foreground

Add one or two larger, darker trunks. Add leaf shapes, pastel marks or bark texture where they help your idea.

Check: can you see foreground, middle ground and background? Keep or change an arrangement for a reason.

Share: “I chose ___ to make ___ seem closer/farther away, or to create a feeling of ___.” Point, speak, draw, dictate or write.

Teacher preparation · about 70 minutes

Notice and sketch 10 minutes; background 15; layers 30; refine and share 15. Model a size/overlap comparison before students start. Assess intentional visual choices and explanations, with several successful arrangements possible. Offer pre-cut shapes, fewer colours, drawing or a partner for cutting.
