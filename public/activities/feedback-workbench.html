Build a complete, personal comment

Strength + evidence + next step:
You [specific demonstrated skill], as shown when you [precise example]. Next, try [one concrete action] so that [learning purpose].

Add a support or thinking question when it genuinely helps. Use a learner’s name only in the approved destination. Avoid generic praise, invented improvement, personality judgments and promises about a future proficiency level.

Adapt the wording to the evidence
  • Supported work: “Using the worked example, you…” Describe the support without treating access supports as automatic reasons to lower achievement.
  • Developing explanation: “Your diagram shows… Next, explain why…” Do not turn a writing or language barrier into an unsupported claim about subject knowledge.
  • Limited evidence: “In our check-in, you…” State the limits. Ask for a short demonstration rather than inventing a full assessment.
  • Revision: “In the revised version, you changed…” Compare only dated, comparable evidence.
  • Extension: Ask the learner to transfer, justify, generalize or examine a limitation, not merely do more questions.

Before posting in SpacesEDU
  • Original evidence read, including later comments/pages when in scope. Unreadable or missing material identified.
  • Learning goal and actual criteria match the comment. Only demonstrated competencies are discussed.
  • Strength has a specific example; next step is doable and addresses this learner’s work.
  • Work completed with supports is described accurately. No diagnosis or private information is exposed unnecessarily.
  • Teacher-selected proficiency and comment are consistent. Accuracy percentages are not automatic proficiency conversions.
  • Correct student, assignment and audience checked. Draft/review status is clear; publication occurs only when authorized.
  • Saved or posted outcome verified before moving to the next item. Preserve the original when revising if the workflow requires it.


Make the next check small
Choose one: explain a step orally; annotate one diagram; solve a parallel example; find one better source; revise one sentence; demonstrate the skill with the usual supports.
Separate the questions: “What does this learner understand?” is different from “What has been uploaded?” and “What support helps them show it?”
