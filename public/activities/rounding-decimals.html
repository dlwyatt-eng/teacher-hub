Round a decimal. Explain what changes.

Use paper or a whiteboard. We are choosing a nearby value at a named place, while keeping track of the original measurement.

Model · nearest hundredth

7.264 is between 7.26 and 7.27.

The halfway point is 7.265. Our number is below halfway, so the nearer hundredth is 7.26.

The thousandths digit is 4: it tells us to keep the hundredths digit. For these positive numbers, use the classroom convention that an exact halfway value rounds to the higher neighbour. So 7.265 rounds to 7.27.

The original value stays 7.264; 7.26 is its rounded value. A number line or place-value chart is available if it helps your explanation.

Your turn

  1. Round 6.284 to the nearest tenth. Name the two neighbouring tenths, then explain which is nearer.
  2. Round 2.305 to the nearest hundredth. Explain how the halfway convention applies.
  3. A measurement is 3.47 m. Someone reports it as 3.5 m. Describe a situation where that precision is useful and one where you would want the original measurement.

Independent check: round 4.376 to the nearest hundredth. Show the place that helps you decide. Hand in or explain this check as directed.

Checking guidance · open after trying

6.284 → 6.3 (between 6.2 and 6.3, above 6.25). 2.305 → 2.31 (halfway between 2.30 and 2.31). 4.376 → 4.38 (thousandths 6, above 4.375).

A rough room-size estimate may use 3.5 m; cutting a precisely fitted piece may require 3.47 m or a more precise measurement. Judge the explanation against the purpose, not a single required context.
