Orange Shirt Day art examples

Everyone can begin today. You do not need to finish another booklet first. Your artwork can continue another day.

We listened to an artistic retelling of Chanie Wenjack’s story. Orange Shirt Day began with Phyllis Webstad sharing her own, different experience. Return to Phyllis’s story and what we have learned about residential schools. Choose a design that helps you explain why children matter, why listening matters, or why identities and belonging must be respected.

Our question: What do we want people to understand and remember through our artwork?

Make your meaning clear

  1. Choose a simple symbol and the meaning you want it to carry: a heart, book, speech bubbles, a circle of hearts, a home or a seedling.
  2. Sketch on your paper shirt or plain paper. Add clear outlines and a short message. You can point to choices, use large shapes or ask for help with cutting or writing.
  3. Explain one choice: “I chose ___ to show ___. This connects to Phyllis’s story / our learning because ___.” Point, speak, draw, dictate or write; someone can record your words.

Example: An open book with “Listen and learn” can show a commitment to hearing a Survivor’s own account. Say whose account helped you think of it.

Use your own design. Learn about specific Indigenous artworks from their artists rather than copying cultural symbols.
